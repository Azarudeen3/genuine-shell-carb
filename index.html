THE SCIENCE

Understanding Activated Carbon

1

Introduction

Activated carbon can be made from coal, coconut shells and wood. The material is charred to achieve carbon, followed by chemical activation or high temperature steam activation — producing an extensive network of pores with surface areas of 300 to 2000 m²/g. The pores provide sites for adsorption of chemical contaminants in gases or liquids.

2

Activation

Material such as wood, coconut shells or coal is carbonized at approximately 450°C in an oxygen-free atmosphere, driving off volatile organic compounds and leaving behind carbon and minerals (ash).

3

Steam Activation

The charcoal is then activated at up to 1000°C in a steam atmosphere. This thermal process creates millions of micropores, expanding the surface area exponentially.

STEAM ACTIVATION PROCESS
C + H2O → CO + H2
